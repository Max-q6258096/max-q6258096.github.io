Max Cane Oct/31/25 I thought that it was interesting that Motherhood is Crisis chose a web comic as its medium to deliver its message. I think that overall this method worked at creating a statement, informing the reading, and telling a story, but it could have been done much better. I didn’t like the art style the illustrator used, it looked cheap and uninspired and lacked style or emotion. I couldn’t tell if the illustrator traced over photographs to create the panels of the web comic but it seemed like they did. I think the lack of quality in the art of the illustrations took away from the impact of the narrative it was trying to exhibit. There were times when the web comic transitioned to real photographs with text bubbles over them, I thought this was a much more interesting and informative way to tell a story then using the poorly drawn web comic panels. The photographs felt very authentic and made me feel more attached to the story than the low quality drawings. If the drawings were made by the women or others involved in the story (which they may have been, I am not sure because I couldn’t find where the artist or author was credited) I think that the drawings would have been effective. But as is, the photographs with text were a complete improvement to the narrative and ability to give information to the reader then the drawings and I think it would have been much more effective to have the web comic be made with the photography and text format rather than the digital illustrations they used.
