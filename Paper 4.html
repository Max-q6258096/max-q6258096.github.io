Max Cane December 12 2025 I think that there are a lot of complicated factors around how we as both individuals and collective members of society approach the overwhelming challenge of disposable plastic and its catastrophic effects on the environment. The larger issues around day to day recycling are the various types of plastic that are recyclable and non-recyclable, the different symbols attached to those plastics, and the realistic expectation of the average consumer to take the time to properly dispose of those plastics by examining the plastic objects and reading the correct information about it. I think the article did a decent job at pointing out how this is a problem that is too big to be solved at the individual level and that the only way to possibly solve it is through holding the companies directly responsible and through larger government programs that affect mass communities and areas. I think that regulating and banning most single use plastics is something that is absolute in order to solve issues around plastic pollution. The amount of plastic used in everyday objects by millions of people is insurmountable in scale. Plastic straws, plastic bags, and other objects made of plastic based materials used in packaging and shipping are overwhelming, however they are all also objects that can be replaced by non-plastic materials and likely be functionally swapped out for materials that are sustainable. Changing and replacing single-use plastics for other materials that are sustainable and other legislation around pollution are changes that are possible to achieve at a significant level within many countries like China, Japan, and various countries in the European Union due to their ability to implement functional regulation and legislative changes at a realistic rate. The United States is not a country that has a legislative system that functions at a level where change can be incorporated at a mass scale due to the effect of corporate lobbying and the inability for congress, the senate, and the supreme court to function as intended in regard to passing and creating new laws and legislation. I think that the article spends too much time at the end focusing on what the reader can do as an individual in regard to the types of products we consume and buy based on their sustainability and ability to be recycled as even if the reader is to perfectly recycle all of their plastics correctly it will have any effect as many sources report that over 75% of recycled plastic in the U.S. will end up in landfills. I think realistically the only thing the average American can do to significantly impact the issue is by trying to work on a larger scale level to impact their local government to implement legislations and laws around these issues that can affect the corporations that produce these plastic materials. I think that generally America will never be a leader in any cause around climate change or recycling. President Trump has already announced he is going to remove America from the Paris Climate Agreement for a second time at some point in 2026. Many republican senators, congressmen, and other elected officials have spoken out against and outright mocked the concept of banning plastic straws in recent years. Few democrats have made significant effort to improve recycling or create any tax related program that would create more opportunities for larger corporations to reduce their use of non-sustainable materials.
